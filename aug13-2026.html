Deal Structure

Offer: 0.3463 Curaleaf shares + US$0.75 cash per Aurora share = US$4.00/share. Consideration capped at US$5.00/share (Jordan confirmed). Aurora board has declined twice previously without engaging. Jordan has set ~Aug 18 as the deadline before launching a formal hostile shareholder tender — bypassing the board entirely.

Strategic Rationale

Aurora Cannabis holds 50+ ton/year EU-GMP certified cultivation capacity across Canada and Europe. This is Curaleaf's primary entry point into the European and international medical cannabis markets, which are growing rapidly as Germany and other EU countries legalize. The deal would create the first truly global cannabis operator by revenue.

Aurora's Situation

Aurora has ~$7M/quarter cash burn and high SG&A relative to peers. The 110% premium to net cash (stripping Aurora's balance sheet) implies the market values Aurora's operations near zero — making the bid compelling to shareholders even if the board resists.

Industry Data — Ohio & Earnings

Aug 13 Verified — TDR/Primary

Ohio Licensed Cannabis: $107M April Record + 26% YoY After Hemp Ban

Ohio adult-use market hit $107M in April 2026 (all-time record), +26% YoY. After Ohio's hemp enforcement action, licensed dispensaries saw +9% sales per day — documented proof that hemp bans drive consumers to licensed stores. Multiple MSO CEOs cite Ohio as the Nov 12 template.

Read more

The Ohio Data

Ohio's adult-use market launched in 2024. At its 2-year mark (Aug 2026): $107M in April 2026 sales, all-time monthly record; +26% year-over-year growth. After Ohio enforcement action against hemp-derived THC products, licensed dispensaries immediately saw +9% per day in sales — the direct consumer shift effect is now quantified.

Why This Matters for Nov 12

Ohio is being cited industry-wide as the template for what happens when a state eliminates the hemp THC market. Multiple MSO CEOs (TerrAscend, Jushi, Curaleaf) referenced Ohio data in Q2 2026 earnings calls to explain why they expect licensed-market growth to accelerate nationally after Nov 12. If the federal pattern mirrors Ohio: consumers don't disappear — they shift to licensed stores.

Legislation — What's Real vs. What Isn't

Aug 13 NOT Real Legislation Verified — GovTrack + PDF

"Full-Spectrum CBD and Hemp Clarification Act of 2026" — Lawyer's Draft, Not a Bill

CLN published a video called "The CBD Bill Nobody Is Talking About." It is not a bill before Congress — it's an advocacy draft written by cannabis attorney Thomas E. Howard (the CLN host himself). No bill number, no sponsor, no committee referral. Verified via GovTrack (0 results) and PDF metadata.

Read more

What Was Verified

GovTrack API query for the 119th Congress: 0 results for "Full-Spectrum CBD and Hemp Clarification." The PDF hosted on cannabisindustrylawyer.com contains decisive metadata: Author = Thomas E. Howard; Subject = "Prepared for legislative consideration; not introduced legislation"; Creator = "Cannabis Industry Lawyer outreach package." The bill-format caption ("119th CONGRESS / 2d Session / A BILL") is drafting style, not evidence of introduction.

What the Draft Would Do

Amends the Agricultural Marketing Act of 1946 to protect full-spectrum CBD products — while explicitly excluding "intoxicating and synthetic hemp-derived products." The exact definition of "intoxicating" is not provided in the available text, which is the critical issue. If this language were ever enacted, intoxicating hemp-derived THC products would fall in the exclusion — the draft offers them no protection.

Real Introduced Bills (for comparison)

  • H.R. 9830 — Lawful Hemp Protection Act (introduced Jul 22, 2026 — 0 cosponsors, no committee action)
  • H.R. 9913 — Safe Sips Act of 2026 (hemp beverages only — inert)
  • Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — Van Duyne/Landsman (beverages only, 5mg/serving — introduced Aug 10)
  • H.R. 7212 — HEMP Act (introduced, no floor action)

None of these are on a path to passing before Nov 12. Congressional rescue remains off the table.

Directional Signal

Even as an unintroduced draft, this proposal reflects where some in Congress are thinking: protect therapeutic CBD, carve out intoxicating products. That's consistent with Dr. Oz's Medicare CBD position and the synthetic cannabinoid permanent carve-out in P.L. 119-37. The policy trajectory is not favorable to intoxicating hemp-derived THC products long-term.

Jul 29 CCB Board ⏰ Comment Deadline ~Aug 28 Official — ccb.vermont.gov

CCB Draft Advertising Guidance — 4 Material Changes, Comment Window Open

CCB posted a draft updated Advertising Guidance for 30-day public comment (~closes Aug 28, 2026). Four material changes — including a brand-new AI advertising rule. Marketing team has completed a full review.

Read more — all 4 changes

1. 🆕 AI-Assisted Advertising — NEW RULE (High Relevance)

First-ever AI content subsection in CCB Advertising Guidance. Three requirements when using AI to generate advertising content:

  • No misleading product images (AI cannot generate images that misrepresent product appearance)
  • No modified health warning symbols
  • Exact required health warning text must appear — AI cannot paraphrase or rewrite it

Any AI-assisted workflow used for social or marketing concepts needs checking against these three requirements. The burden is low where AI content stays at concept/draft stage, but the requirements become enforceable once the guidance is final.

2. Age-Gating — Clarification (Medium Relevance)

The draft strengthens age-gating language: the gate must fire if visitors go DIRECTLY TO A SUB-PAGE, not just the homepage. Many licensee sites won't handle sub-page direct entry out of the box — a technical check is needed. The public comment window is an opportunity to ask the CCB for implementation guidance on hosted e-commerce platforms (Shopify, Squarespace, Weebly, etc.).

3. Social Media Health Warning — Pinned Comment (Language Update)

The Feb 2026 version said the warning may be "the first comment that follows." The new draft says the method must "guarantee the post and warning are actually seen together on ALL DEVICES AND PLATFORMS through which the post might be rendered." This platform-portability test is more stringent and may affect whether Instagram/Facebook pinning satisfies the requirement. Seek clarity via public comment.

4. Prizes, Awards & Inducements — Statutory Clarification (Already in Effect)

Codifies Act 166 of 2023 (effective June 10, 2024) — spin-a-wheel prizes at checkout, loyalty programs, and merchandise-with-purchase are now PERMITTED for adult-use retail. This is not new — it's been law since Jun 2024. The explicit inclusion in Advertising Guidance confirms licensees can run prize and loyalty promotions with pre-approval.

How to Submit Comment

Visit ccb.vermont.gov/guidance, find the draft Advertising Guidance (July 2026 markup), and follow the comment submission instructions. Deadline is approximately August 28, 2026 (30 days from July 29 Board meeting).

Ongoing Vermont Context

Vermont Already Stricter Than Federal

Vermont adopted its own intoxicating hemp ban with a 10mg/package cap effective July 1, 2026. Licensed Vermont operators are already working under that state framework. The federal Nov 12 deadline adds a layer on top of what Vermont has already done.

Read more

Vermont's Hemp Framework

Vermont CCB adopted an intoxicating hemp ban effective July 1, 2026 with a 10mg/package total THC cap. Delta-8, delta-9-from-hemp, and similar manufactured intoxicating cannabinoids are subject to Vermont's licensing system. Unlicensed hemp THC products have been non-compliant in Vermont since July 1 — the state ran about a month ahead of the federal deadline.

How Federal Nov 12 Adds to Vermont's Framework

Vermont's rule covers intrastate sales and licensing. The federal P.L. 119-37 adds a federal layer: any hemp product with more than 0.4mg total THC per container becomes a controlled substance federally on Nov 12 (or Dec 11 if the CR delay passes and House concurs). Interstate commerce and federal enforcement are the new dimensions. Vermont-only sales under the licensed framework remain governed by Vermont's rules.

The Open Question for Licensees

Whether a given input or hemp SKU meets the federal "synthetic or converted cannabinoid" definition is the decisive question — those products hit the Nov 12 hard stop permanently, regardless of whether the CR delay passes. It is an attorney question, not a reading-the-statute one.
