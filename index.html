Federal Hemp & THC Ban

Aug 14–15, 2026 Beverages Only No Gummy Protection Verified — Primary

Beverage Regulatory Parity Act (BRPA) — Bipartisan Bill Introduced; No Protection for Edibles

Reps. Van Duyne (R-TX) and Landsman (D-OH) introduced the BRPA on Aug 10, proposing a three-tier alcohol-style framework for hemp THC beverages capped at 5mg/serving. The bill covers beverages only — it explicitly provides zero protection for edibles, gummies, capsules, or inhalables. The alcohol-industry backers (WSWA, BAMCO) framed it as accepting the broader hemp ban on synthetics while carving out beverages. Industry media is now treating "beverages will survive regardless" as conventional wisdom, which by inversion confirms gummies and edibles sit on the unprotected side of the policy split.

Read more

Key BRPA Provisions

Adults 21+ may purchase hemp THC beverages; max 5mg intoxicating THC per serving; no multi-serving containers over 750ml; products must contain only naturally-occurring US-cultivated cannabinoids (synthetics excluded even from the beverage carve-out); federal excise tax of 8 cents per milligram of THC; TTB/HHS/USDA oversight; interstate commerce cannot be blocked by states.

What BRPA Does Not Cover

Edibles, gummies, chocolates, baked goods, capsules, inhalables — none protected. BAMCO's framing explicitly accepts the ban on synthetics/inhalables while asking only for beverages. WSWA is a beverage organization and has no mandate for food/edibles.

DEA / HHC Reinforcing Signal (Aug 13)

Same week: DEA defended its Schedule I classification of HHC (a synthetic/converted cannabinoid) in court, arguing hemp companies challenging it "lack standing." DEA said pausing enforcement would be "administratively burdensome." This reinforces that synthetic/converted cannabinoids have no protection pathway, even through litigation.

Legislative Odds

GovTrack H.R.6500 (CR with Dec 11 delay): 34% prognosis — House returns from recess ~Aug 31. BRPA itself is a standalone bill with no hearing scheduled; odds of standalone passage in 119th Congress are low.

Sources: Marijuana Moment Aug 10 & Aug 11, 2026 (primary); Marijuana Moment Aug 13, 2026 (DEA/HHC)

Federal Rescheduling Track

Aug 16–18, 2026 Milestone Reached Verified — Official

Rescheduling: Final Briefs Filed Aug 18 — Evidentiary Record Now Closed

DEA and all opposition parties filed their final post-hearing briefs on August 18. The evidentiary record is fully closed. ALJ Derek Julius will now draft his non-binding Recommended Decision (estimated ~mid-November 2026). DEA's brief explicitly argued cannabis "no longer fits Schedule I requirements," cited 30,000+ practitioners and 6 million+ patients in 43 jurisdictions, and asked ALJ Julius to "expeditiously recommend" Schedule III. Opposition arguments included a new procedural challenge from the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (AG-issued rule vs. DEA Administrator authority) and a new institutional actor — NDASA (drug testing industry) — arguing that Schedule III would eliminate federal drug testing authority for safety-sensitive workers.

Read more

DEA's Position (Pro-Rescheduling)

DEA's brief: cannabis no longer meets Schedule I criteria because it has "currently accepted medical use" and "accepted safety under medical supervision." Cited 30,000+ practitioners treating 6M+ patients in 43 jurisdictions. Asked ALJ to "expeditiously recommend" Sch III transfer.

Opposition Arguments

SAM + Idaho/Indiana/Nebraska: Attacked the new two-part CAMU test as "novel standards dreamt up only for this proceeding." Argued the established 5-part test must be used and fails for cannabis.
Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI) — NEW: Procedural challenge arguing the rule was issued by the Attorney General, not DEA Administrator — a legal hook that, if accepted, could block rescheduling on procedure alone.
NDASA (drug testing industry) — NEW ACTOR: Argued Sch III would end federal drug testing authority for cannabis for safety-sensitive workers (air traffic controllers, airline pilots, truck drivers, etc.).

What Happens Next

ALJ Julius reviews all briefs and drafts his Recommended Decision — non-binding on DEA Administrator Terry Cole. Cole makes the final call. Then a final rule publishes in the Federal Register with a 30-day effective date. Separately, the Acting AG Blanche interim order (April 22, 2026) already moved state-licensed medical cannabis to Sch III — but the SAM APA lawsuit (filed May 6, 2026) seeks to reverse that.

Note: Separate from Nov 12

This rescheduling track is entirely separate from P.L.119-37's Nov 12 hemp-THC deadline. Do not conflate them.

Schedule III & Medical Channel

Aug 16, 2026 Attorney Q — Unresolved Vermont-Specific Verified — CCB Primary

Vermont Medical-use Endorsement & DOJ Final Order — Key Question Unresolved

The DOJ Final Order of April 22, 2026 moved state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III (Sch I adult-use remains unchanged). Vermont already has a Medical-use Endorsement system (6 V.S.A. §907(f)) layered onto adult-use retailer licenses — endorsed retailers can sell tax-free to registered patients, sell products otherwise prohibited to non-medical customers, and deliver to patients. Whether Vermont's endorsement structure constitutes a "qualifying state-issued medical marijuana license" under the DOJ Final Order — and whether endorsed retailers could benefit from 280E relief on medical sales — is an unresolved attorney and CPA question. Illinois passed an analogous state-level path (applications open Sept 10), showing Vermont is structurally ahead but faces the same federal-recognition uncertainty.

Read more

What the DOJ Final Order Covers

Schedule III covers: (a) FDA-approved products containing marijuana, and (b) marijuana subject to a qualifying state-issued medical marijuana license. Adult-use without medical endorsement stays Schedule I. Legal authority used: 21 U.S.C. §811(d)(1) — not court-tested; SAM APA lawsuit pending.

Vermont Medical-use Endorsement (CCB-Verified)

Vermont's endorsement system (ccb.vermont.gov/medicalendorsement, 6 V.S.A. §907(f)): endorsed adult-use retailers can sell tax-free to registered patients, sell products otherwise prohibited to non-medical customers per Board rulemaking, sell in excess of single-transaction limits, and deliver to registered patients. The structural elements are favorable indicators — but Vermont's endorsement is a layer on an adult-use license, not a standalone medical license. Whether the DOJ distinction tracks this structure is uncertain pending IRS/DOJ guidance.

Sources: ccb.vermont.gov/medicalendorsement (official); cannabisindustrylawyer.com/schedule-iii/; Marijuana Moment Aug 13, 2026

Consumer & Market Intelligence

Aug 18–19, 2026 Market Signal — High Verified — NuggMD Primary Strategy Relevant

56% of Hemp-Ban Consumers Moved to Regulated Market — NuggMD Poll

NuggMD (cannabis telehealth platform) surveyed 583 cannabis consumers in states with active hemp-THC bans (conducted July 16–20, 2026). Result: 50% obtained a medical cannabis card in response to bans; 6% switched to licensed dispensaries — a net 56% migrating into the regulated market. Only 8% reported trouble finding products. Corroborating data (CannIntel, undisclosed sponsor — treat as directional): licensed dispensary revenue up ~11% in ban states six months post-enforcement; Minnesota +14% after Jan 2025 ban. The federal Nov 12 deadline will trigger the same migration nationally — including in Vermont — for all remaining hemp-THC consumers.

Confidence Caveats

NuggMD is a med-card telehealth platform — potential publication bias toward med-card uptake. Sample is consumers in regulated states with dispensary access — may overstate national conversion rate. CannIntel's +11% dispensary revenue figure has an undisclosed sponsor — treat as directional signal, not confirmed data.

Vermont Context

Vermont has an adult-use market (since 2022) and ~3,043 registered medical patients. No Vermont-specific data on hemp-consumer migration is available yet. The federal Nov 12 deadline will force the same migration nationally.

NY Investor Control Precedent (Watch — Not Binding)

CLN reported (Aug 18) on New York's "Sole Control" / True Party of Interest doctrine — longstanding OCM regulation requiring the controlling owner to have unencumbered decision-making authority, with specific rules on passive investors, profit-sharing arrangements, and board control. This is New York law and does not bind Vermont operators, but is noted as a drafting-discipline model: investor or co-ownership paperwork should be checked for investor-protective terms (vetoes, triggered recusal, board control) that cannabis regulators treat as control indicators.
